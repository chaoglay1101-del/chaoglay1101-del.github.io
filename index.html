About

Accounting and finance training, applied through data

I am interested in how digital finance changes the way banks earn money, and in doing that analysis honestly.

My degree background is accounting and finance: financial reporting, corporate finance, and financial markets. The question I kept returning to is what happens to a bank's income structure when payments move from branches and cards into mobile and internet channels.

To answer it concretely, I completed an empirical project rather than an argument from intuition. I assembled a bank-level panel, joined a country-year payment indicator, and estimated bank and year fixed-effects models with bank-clustered standard errors. The result is a conditional association, not a causal claim, and the public documentation says so explicitly.

Alongside the econometrics I work on the parts that make research reusable: a reproducible Python environment, documented variable definitions, a synthetic example that anyone can run, and Tableau stories that make findings legible to a non-technical reader.

Research

Digital payment pressure and bank net fee margins

A final-year panel study of 39 commercial banks in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand, 2014–2022.

Research question

Is digital payment pressure associated with bank net fee margins in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand?

What I did

  1. Constructed bank-level net fee margins and financial controls from a restricted bank panel.
  2. Extracted IMF Financial Access Survey indicator IMF_FAS_FCMIBT: mobile and internet banking transactions as a percentage of GDP.
  3. Merged the country-year measure into the bank-year panel.
  4. Estimated bank and year fixed-effects models with bank-clustered standard errors and 1st/99th percentile winsorisation.
  5. Tested log, interaction, nonlinear, and country-subsample specifications.
  • Python
  • pandas
  • linearmodels
  • PanelOLS
  • IMF FAS
  • WRDS-derived panel

What I found

  • Linear specifications provide limited evidence of a direct association.
  • The log specification shows a negative, statistically significant pooled association.
  • Country subsamples are heterogeneous, and decomposition suggests cross-country structure may drive much of the pooled result.

These are conditional associations. The explanatory measure varies at the country-year level, only three countries are covered, and omitted factors cannot be ruled out.
