Research

Digital payment pressure and bank net fee margins

A final-year panel study of 39 commercial banks in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand, 2014–2022.

Research question

Is digital payment pressure associated with bank net fee margins in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand?

What I did

  1. Constructed bank-level net fee margins and financial controls from a restricted bank panel.
  2. Extracted IMF Financial Access Survey indicator IMF_FAS_FCMIBT: mobile and internet banking transactions as a percentage of GDP.
  3. Merged the country-year measure into the bank-year panel.
  4. Estimated bank and year fixed-effects models with bank-clustered standard errors and 1st/99th percentile winsorisation.
  5. Tested log, interaction, nonlinear and country-subsample specifications, then checked the result with Driscoll–Kraay errors, a Mundlak decomposition, a one-year lag and a net-interest-margin placebo.
  • Python
  • pandas
  • linearmodels
  • PanelOLS
  • IMF FAS
  • WRDS-derived panel

What I found

  • Linear specifications provide limited evidence of a direct association.
  • The log specification shows a negative, statistically significant pooled association.
  • Country subsamples are heterogeneous, and decomposition suggests cross-country structure may drive much of the pooled result.
  • The design tests two competing hypotheses, substitution (Baumol, 1982) and complementarity (Thakor, 2020); the evidence is consistent with substitution in the pooled sample only.

These are conditional associations. The explanatory measure varies at the country-year level, only three countries are covered, and omitted factors cannot be ruled out.

Read the full research page

Because the bank panel is restricted, the thesis regressions are documented and inspectable but cannot be rerun publicly. The repository ships a deterministic synthetic panel so that the estimation workflow still runs end to end.

    Business Analytics module · individual Tableau assignment

    Tableau assignment 70/100 (module mark 70/100 on transcript) · equal to the cohort's upper quartile (70); cohort mean 63.7, maximum 76

    • Prepared the data first: an extract of 437 coded World Bank Findex fields across 174 economies and 12 population groups was mapped down to 24 business-readable measures, with decimals rescaled to percentages and missing binary usage flags imputed as zero.
    • Dropped the 2011 and 2022 waves because their coverage is too thin, leaving 171 economies and four waves (2014, 2017, 2021, 2024).
    • Built five interactive dashboards framed by a cover and a conclusion — Mobile Money Boom, Leapfrogging the Plastic Era, The Catch-Up, The Usage Paradox, and Platform Transformation — each with a written insight and a commercial recommendation.
    • Findings: mobile money adoption expanded steadily from 2014 and leads in Sub-Saharan Africa; digital payment use runs ahead of debit-card ownership; savings lag far behind payment usage, most sharply in lower-income economies; and mobile money drives women's financial participation more than traditional banking does.
    • Wrote a critical assessment of every dashboard, naming the limits of the data — no transaction-level detail, no qualitative evidence — and the chart types that would have carried the message better.
    • Submitted December 2025. Its finding that digital payment use runs ahead of debit-card ownership raised the question my final-year project then tested: what happens to banks' fee income as payments move away from cards.
