1Who I am

Accounting and finance graduate, doing empirical research on digital payments

BSc (Hons) Accounting and Finance from the University of Birmingham, taught in English at its Singapore campus: Class II (Division I), GPA 3.25, with the degree to be conferred on 12 November 2026. I am seeking graduate roles in financial data analysis and master's study in finance or business analytics.

2Where the question came from

A coursework finding became my research question

In my Business Analytics Tableau story, submitted in December 2025, digital payment use ran ahead of debit-card ownership. That raised the question my final-year project tested: what happens to banks' fee income as payments move away from cards?

3What I studied

A bank panel I built and estimated myself

39 commercial banks in Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand, 344 bank-year observations, 2014–2022. I built the Python pipeline, merged in the IMF digital-payment indicator, and estimated bank and year fixed-effects models with bank-clustered standard errors. The research report scored 80/100, above the cohort's upper quartile.

4What it found

A negative association, stated with its limits

The log model shows a negative, statistically significant pooled association between digital payment pressure and net fee margins. The decomposition suggests it comes mainly from differences between countries, so I report a conditional association, not a causal effect.

See the findings step by step on the research page

5Practice

Tax advisory internship: evidence and procedures

At Evershine CPAs, from July to September 2025, I worked on Philippines tax incentive applications, preparing and checking supporting documentation, including BOI Form 501. I also mapped the procedure into SOP flowcharts that the team used for the workflow and for handover.

6What next

From associations to identification

In master's study I want to test designs that can identify an effect: event studies around staggered payment-licensing reforms, inference suited to few clusters, and fee income broken down into payments, cards and merchant services.

    Research experience

    • 2025–2026 · Final-year project

      Digital payment pressure and bank net fee margins — ASEAN-3 bank panel

      Accounting and Finance Research Project · University of Birmingham

      Research report component: 80/100 (65% of module; module total 77/100 on transcript) · above the component's upper quartile (73), i.e. top 25%; cohort mean 67, maximum 84

      • Designed the study around an explicit research question and defined the estimation sample: 39 listed commercial banks, 344 bank-year observations, 2014–2022.
      • Built a Python pipeline to prepare the bank panel, discover and extract the IMF Financial Access Survey indicator IMF_FAS_FCMIBT, and merge the country-year measure into the bank-year panel.
      • Constructed Net_Fee_Margin and financial controls, winsorised continuous variables at the 1st and 99th percentiles, and formed a complete-case estimation sample.
      • Estimated bank and year fixed-effects models with bank-clustered standard errors using linearmodels, then tested log, bank-size interaction, nonlinear and country-subsample specifications; checked the result with Driscoll–Kraay errors, a Mundlak decomposition, a one-year lag and a net-interest-margin placebo.
      • Reported results as conditional associations, stating the data-level, coverage, and omitted-variable limitations explicitly.
      • Published a public research report, variable definitions, and a methodology appendix, plus a runnable synthetic fixed-effects example with an automated test.

    • Business Analytics coursework

      Mobile money adoption in the Global South — Tableau story

      Business Analytics module · University of Birmingham

      Tableau assignment 70/100 (module mark 70/100 on transcript) · equal to the cohort's upper quartile (70); cohort mean 63.7, maximum 76

      • Cleaned and documented the dataset in a data-cleaning log, then designed an interactive Tableau story covering adoption trends, regional comparison, and inclusion drivers.
      • Presented the findings as a management narrative and published the story on Tableau Public.
      • Mapped 437 coded World Bank Findex fields across 174 economies and 12 population groups into 24 business-readable measures, then excluded the 2011 and 2022 waves for coverage, leaving 171 economies and four survey waves (2014–2024).
      • Findings: mobile money adoption leads in Sub-Saharan Africa; digital payment use runs ahead of debit-card ownership; savings lag far behind payment usage; and mobile money drives women's financial participation more than traditional banking does.
      • Submitted December 2025. Its finding that digital payment use runs ahead of debit-card ownership raised the question my final-year project then tested: what happens to banks' fee income as payments move away from cards.
