Web studio, co-founded

TechStarterWeb: a studio website that is also its order desk

TechStarterWeb Consulting builds personal and small-business websites for clients in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore. I co-founded it in October 2026 and lead the technical side. The studio's own site, live since 23 October 2026, is its first piece of work.

Astro · Cloudflare Workers · Cloudflare D1 · Cloudflare Access · OpenCC

What I built

  • One site serving both sides: the public pages take enquiries, and a back office behind sign-in turns each enquiry into an order with a status, internal notes and a record of who changed what.
  • Three languages: Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese generated from it at build time with OpenCC, and English pages for Singapore. Visitors are sent to their browser's language, and the contact options follow the region: LINE, WhatsApp, WeChat and Xiaohongshu.
  • Privacy built in: the back office admits only the team's addresses, through Cloudflare Access with one-time email codes; the server checks the sign-in again; and a client's record can be deleted for good on request.
  • Launch and operations: the techstarterweb.com domain, a www redirect, a hello@ mailbox, Google Search Console with a sitemap, and visit statistics that count a page only after a click, a scroll or ten seconds on screen, so scanners are left out.

What it does not do yet

Enquiries are not yet emailed to the team; they are read in the back office. Cloudflare's free plan has no servers in mainland China, so some mainland networks load the site slowly or not at all. The English pages are translated by hand and can fall behind the Chinese.

How an enquiry moves

  1. Enquiry A client fills in the form in any of the three languages.
  2. Stored A hidden field turns away basic spam, and the enquiry is stored in the D1 database.
  3. Sign-in A team member signs in to the back office with an email code.
  4. Status The order moves from enquiry to quoted, signed, in production and delivered, or is marked as not going ahead. Enquiry Quoted Signed In production Delivered
  5. Logged Every change is logged with who made it and when.

How the work is organised

  • State lives in GitHub, not in chat: each task card lists its goal, the files it may change, pre-assigned version numbers and acceptance checks, and every session ends with a four-part handoff note.
  • Rules became checks: CI fails a pull request that edits files outside its card, reuses a version number, or changes code without updating the spec.
  • Cost control: routine work goes to cheaper models, every model call is logged, and ten calls to the low-cost model came to about US$0.03.

How it was built

I built it with an AI coding agent (Claude). I set the requirements and the architecture, including the idea of one site serving both clients and the studio; made the account, security and launch decisions; reviewed each change; and checked it on the live site. The agent wrote the code, and each release was tested before it went out. The workflow is open-sourced, with tests, as layered-ai-dev-workflow.

Business Analytics coursework

Mobile money adoption in the Global South

An individual Tableau story, submitted in December 2025: five dashboards on how mobile money is spreading across the Global South, built from World Bank Global Findex and GSMA data.

Tableau · Excel · Python · World Bank Findex and GSMA data

What I did

  • Prepared the data first: an extract of 437 coded World Bank Findex fields across 174 economies and 12 population groups was mapped down to 24 business-readable measures, with decimals rescaled to percentages and missing binary usage flags imputed as zero.
  • Dropped the 2011 and 2022 waves because their coverage is too thin, leaving 171 economies and four waves (2014, 2017, 2021, 2024).
  • Built five interactive dashboards framed by a cover and a conclusion — Mobile Money Boom, Leapfrogging the Plastic Era, The Catch-Up, The Usage Paradox, and Platform Transformation — each with a written insight and a commercial recommendation.
  • Wrote a critical assessment of every dashboard, naming the limits of the data — no transaction-level detail, no qualitative evidence — and the chart types that would have carried the message better.

What it found

  • Mobile money adoption expanded steadily from 2014 and leads in Sub-Saharan Africa.
  • Digital payment use runs ahead of debit-card ownership, and savings trail both.
  • Mobile money drives women's financial participation more than traditional banking does.

Where it led

Its finding that digital payment use runs ahead of debit-card ownership raised the question my final-year project then tested: what happens to banks' fee income as payments move away from cards.

layered-ai-dev-workflow: how the studio works with AI coding agents

The layered way of working that TechStarterWeb uses with AI coding agents, open-sourced with tests so that others can reuse or adapt it. The five-layer figure is in the TechStarterWeb project above.

  • A lead agent turns a to-do into a task card, worker agents write the code, a low-cost model handles copy and translation, and scripts and CI check whatever a rule can decide.
  • Work state is kept in GitHub issues and handoff notes rather than in chat, so a new session can pick up where the last one stopped.
  • The rules are enforced by checks: a pull request that edits files outside its task card, reuses a version number, or skips the spec update fails CI.
